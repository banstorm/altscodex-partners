Today a handful of big-tech companies guard the gates of app distribution, and every account and every dollar inside follows their rules. Beside them, we want to build a new path where users own their accounts and everyone who builds the ecosystem shares in it. Accounts belong to users

An account a user has built with their time and money belongs to that user, not the platform. Its ownership should be transferable, it should be delegable, and its history should be on record.

Distribution is owned together

Instead of one gatekeeper setting the fees, the participants who build the ecosystem set the rules and share the revenue.

Build together, share together

Developers, trading platforms, builders and distribution partners set this ecosystem's first standards together and share in its growth as peers.

When supply overflows, value shifts from apps to users. Any app can be replaced, but the accounts, identity, purchases and history users have built up cannot. Yet legacy stores are still stuck displaying and ranking apps. The next distribution channel must put the user's account at the center, not the app, and let that account move freely between apps and people.

An ecosystem where what you buy is yours and the accounts you grow can be transferred

On existing stores, everything users buy and build is locked to the platform. In the new ecosystem, developers allow account transfers from the start and take part in them. Users keep several sub-accounts under one verified identity and transfer the accounts they have grown, with a full record.

These movements ask for one thing: what users buy should belong to them. Legacy stores cannot fully answer that demand, because transferring account ownership was never part of their structure. AltsCodex is built on exactly that structure: accounts that transfer with a permanent record.

A different structure changes how value flows. Every time an account changes hands, its value stays inside the ecosystem instead of leaking out, and is shared by the participants who built this path together.

Why legacy cannot easily follow

Lock-in is their business model

Legacy stores make money by keeping accounts and purchases inside the platform. The moment they open account transfer, they undermine their own control and fee structure.

Designed differently from the start

Existing account systems assume one person, one account, no transfer. AltsCodex was designed from day one around hierarchical accounts, ownership history, escrow and delegation. Bolting on one feature does not close that gap.

On the side of where the world is moving

Law and public opinion on digital ownership are moving toward broader user rights. Legacy players have to defend against that tide. AltsCodex starts on top of it.

Ready for accounts in the AI era

As apps and agents multiply, accounts get created, delegated and moved more than ever. AltsCodex already has the account lifecycle to handle every one of those moments.

We are not playing the legacy stores' game. We do not compete to display more apps. We compete on a different board, one built around the account. Whoever writes the rules of that board first will own the next era of distribution.
